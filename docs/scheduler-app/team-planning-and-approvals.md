# Team content planning and approvals for Simple Post

A four-person brand team should be able to plan December across Instagram, TikTok and LinkedIn, get the founder’s sign-off on the actual copy and media, and publish natively. Build that complete workflow before integrating it with automatic publishing. The first release succeeds when the team can answer three questions from the calendar: what goes out next, who owes the next action, and exactly what did the founder approve?

Shared calendars, a role boundary, post previews and feedback attached to the post recur across the three. Two products document an enforced decision. **Give Simple Post the review convenience of a Postiz link and the explicit decision of a FeedHive or PostFast approval.** Two further requirements come directly from this customer: a named owner with a handoff deadline, and a useful finish line for people who publish in the native apps. A scheduled timestamp answers neither need.

## The first month in Simple Post

1. **The SMM plans the month.** Create a brand workspace, choose its timezone and select Instagram, TikTok and LinkedIn as planning destinations. On the existing month grid, add “Winter launch” to December 8. Start with only a title and intended date. Undated ideas go in a backlog. Selecting a platform requires no social login.
2. **The designer and copywriter hand off one item.** The SMM assigns the designer and sets a December 2 work deadline. The designer attaches a carousel and vertical video, comments on the crop, then hands the item to the copywriter with a December 4 deadline. The copywriter adds the three captions and selects the final media for each. Working files and brief links stay alongside the thread and are clearly distinguished from final assets. One current owner is enough for this handoff. The activity history preserves earlier assignments.
3. **The founder reviews on a phone.** The SMM selects “Request approval”. One email opens a private queue of posts awaiting this founder. Each item shows the intended dates and a caption and media preview for every destination. The founder chooses **Approve** or **Request changes** and identifies the variant in a comment. A change request returns to the owner. The next submission shows what changed and preserves the earlier decision.
4. **The SMM posts natively.** Approved items stay on the calendar. The SMM copies the approved caption, downloads the matching final assets and records “Scheduled natively” or “Published natively” per platform, with an optional permalink. If Instagram is live and TikTok is still waiting, the card shows “1 of 3 published”. A calendar date, approval or manual record triggers no provider call.

| Calendar card and filters | Post detail | Founder’s queue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Title, platform icons, planned date, editorial stage and current owner. Week and month use the same records. Filter by platform, owner or stage. A “Due this week” filter uses work deadlines. | Brief, planned dates, work due date, owner, platform variants, captions, working files, final media, feedback and decision history. Keep time optional for month planning. | Only submitted work for this reviewer. Show what is due soonest, allow a decision followed by “Next”, and send a single email for a batch submission. |

**Permissions for this brand.** The SMM is workspace owner and manages invitations, assignments and submission. The designer and copywriter are contributors who can edit, upload, comment and hand off work. The founder is a reviewer who sees submitted items and their review history, can comment and decide, and needs no billing or channel access. The owner designates the founder as the required approver. Managing the workspace gives the SMM no implicit right to sign for the founder. A replacement approver is visible in the history and must receive a fresh request.

**What the badges mean.** The editorial stages are Draft, In design, In review, Changes requested and Approved. “Scheduled” and “Published” describe a destination, with the source shown as native or Simple Post. Keep the work deadline, intended publication date and actual publication timestamp distinct. A week with a deadline on Wednesday and a launch on Friday should put the launch on Friday and flag the Wednesday handoff.

**Exactly what gets approved.** One decision covers the displayed set of variants. Freeze their destinations, public copy, ordered final assets, content options and displayed publication dates in a review version. A change to any of those fields requires a fresh decision for outstanding destinations. Reassignment, working notes and internal deadlines can change without invalidating it. A stale review link shows the new version and requires a deliberate decision. Preserve the approved version and outcome for a destination already published. If an item was scheduled in a native app, tell the SMM it needs a manual update and reconfirmation there.

## Free entry and pilot decision

**Proposed free pilot:** one brand workspace, three planning platforms, three working members plus one reviewer, and up to 30 active content items with a bounded media allowance. A campaign with three platform variants counts as one item. Archive completed work without losing the decisions. Keep the founder’s review, revisions and native handoff available through a complete monthly cycle. Start by testing paid demand for additional workspaces, more active content or storage, and optional automatic publishing. Treat the limits as hypotheses and measure actual video storage and download costs before setting public pricing.

Recruit five small brand teams already using Sheets and chat. Ask each to bring its next month of real work. Proposed go/no-go signal: at least three teams invite a contributor and a reviewer, finish two review cycles, and return to plan the next month. Measure time from submission to decision, share of reviews resolved in Simple Post, and how many approved variants are marked as posted natively. Ask about off-platform feedback when those numbers diverge. Only prioritize bulk decisions, Slack notifications, CSV import, custom stages or multi-step approvals when observed friction warrants them.

**What the prospect can use today.** Hootsuite offers a free Google Sheets content calendar. Add columns for current owner, work due date, intended post date, platform, caption, final asset link, editorial status, approved version, approver and decision time. Use one row per platform, grouped by campaign. Have the founder review a filtered “In review” list and record the decision on the row. Link to a fixed copy or named revision of the assets and caption. Slack can carry the reminder and row link. If the creative changes, reopen review. This gives the team an immediate workflow and a concrete baseline for testing the Simple Post pilot. [Free calendar template](https://blog.hootsuite.com/social-media-calendar/)

## Implementation decisions, November 24

The creator's active Advanced or Pro entitlement pays for one or three workspaces respectively. Basic and the free trial have no creation allowance. Existing workspaces survive a downgrade. Invited collaborators sign in with the verified email on their invitation and use the team's access. The creator starts as an Admin and Approver. One current workspace approver can decide, including a Viewer or the author.

Reviewers sign off on the title, ordered platform variants, destinations, captions, options, ordered final files and publication time. Assignments, internal deadlines and discussion stay editable. A decision is permanent history after an approver leaves. New decisions always check current membership. Personal posts stay in the personal planner. Teams can plan native publication without connecting accounts. For SimplePost delivery the creator selects a personal connection as a named destination before review, and explicitly releases the approved version. Credentials stay in the existing account and dispatcher systems.

The creator owns the workspace for its lifetime. Removing a collaborator preserves their past attribution. Deleting the creator account removes its workspaces, originals and decisions with the account. Creation requires a qualifying hosted plan, including an active grant of that plan. Self-hosted mode has no paid-plan creation entitlement. Personal delivery copies can be canceled by the account owner and the workspace records their removal. Native and connected releases use separate planning items.

Private uploads support JPEG, PNG, WebP and MP4 up to 8 MiB each, with 512 MiB of original media per workspace. Admins can remove unused uploads. The service retains every file used by a current post or a historical review. A connected release copies the approved media into the creator's existing publishing storage and charges the normal scheduling allowance per platform delivery.

## Activate the private rollout

The migration adds `TEAM_WORKSPACES` to the existing `Feature` enum and creates no grants. For an owner-only launch, leave `TEAM_WORKSPACES` out of `GLOBAL_FEATURES` and grant it to your SimplePost user ID in `user_feature`. Use the [existing grant and revoke procedure](../image-fitting.md#private-hosted-rollout) with `Feature.TEAM_WORKSPACES`. Add another account by granting the same feature to its user ID.

The planner and workspace API check the signed-in account's grant. Collaborators also need a workspace membership or invitation. Workspace creation follows the creator's plan allowance. To roll out to everyone later, add `TEAM_WORKSPACES` to the existing `GLOBAL_FEATURES` list.
